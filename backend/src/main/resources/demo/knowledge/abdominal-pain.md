# Acute abdominal pain (demonstration)

## Overview
The location, onset, character and progression of abdominal pain, together with associated symptoms and examination findings, guide the differential. Pregnancy status is relevant for people who could be pregnant.

## Appendicitis
Appendicitis classically begins with poorly localised periumbilical pain that migrates to the right lower quadrant, often with loss of appetite, nausea and low-grade fever. Raised white cell count and inflammatory markers are common but not universal. Ultrasound or CT may be used depending on the patient and local practice.

## Biliary and pancreatic causes
Cholecystitis often causes right upper quadrant pain, sometimes after meals, with fever and tenderness. Pancreatitis typically causes severe epigastric pain radiating to the back with vomiting; serum lipase or amylase is commonly measured.

## Obstruction and other causes
Bowel obstruction may present with colicky pain, distension, vomiting and absent passage of stool or flatus. Gastroenteritis usually causes diarrhoea and vomiting, often with diffuse cramping pain. Urinary tract infection and renal colic can cause flank or lower abdominal pain with urinary symptoms; urinalysis is commonly performed.

## Information that often changes the assessment
Migration of pain, peritonism on examination, bowel habit, urinary symptoms, pregnancy test result, inflammatory markers and imaging findings are frequently cited as information that helps to distinguish causes.
