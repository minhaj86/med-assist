## Overview
Acute cough with fever and breathlessness is commonly caused by respiratory infection, but non-infective causes such as pulmonary embolism, heart failure and exacerbations of asthma or COPD are also discussed in the evaluation.

## Community-acquired pneumonia
Pneumonia is frequently associated with fever, productive cough, breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, raised respiratory rate and reduced oxygen saturation. Focal chest signs may be present. Raised inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein and white cell count are often seen. A chest X-ray is commonly used to look for consolidation. Severity assessment tools are used in practice; local guidance determines which tool and thresholds apply.

## Viral respiratory infection and acute bronchitis
Influenza and other viral infections may cause fever, myalgia, cough and sore throat. Acute bronchitis typically causes cough without the focal findings associated with pneumonia.

## Non-infective causes to keep in mind
Pulmonary embolism can mimic infection when pleuritic pain, tachycardia and hypoxia are present. Heart failure may cause breathlessness, orthopnoea and leg swelling. Asthma and COPD exacerbations are suggested by wheeze and a history of airway disease.

## Information that often changes the assessment
Duration of symptoms, sputum characteristics, oxygen saturation trend, chest examination findings, chest imaging, recent travel or contact history, immune status and previous lung disease are commonly identified as information that influences the differential.
