## Overview
Chest pain has a broad differential that includes cardiac, vascular, pulmonary, gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal causes. Assessment typically prioritises conditions that can be immediately life-threatening, such as acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary embolism, aortic dissection and tension pneumothorax, before considering more benign explanations.

## Features commonly discussed for acute coronary syndrome
Pressure-like or heavy central chest discomfort, radiation to the arm, jaw or neck, associated sweating (diaphoresis), nausea and breathlessness are features that are frequently described in presentations of acute coronary syndrome. Risk factors often considered include older age, smoking history, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia and a family history of premature coronary disease. Atypical presentations occur, particularly in older adults, women and people with diabetes.

## Investigations frequently used to distinguish cardiac causes
A 12-lead ECG is usually obtained early in the evaluation of acute chest pain. Cardiac troponin testing, often repeated over time according to local protocols, helps to identify myocardial injury. Chest X-ray may help identify alternative explanations such as pneumothorax or pneumonia.

## Pulmonary embolism considerations
Pulmonary embolism may present with pleuritic chest pain, breathlessness, tachycardia, low oxygen saturation or haemoptysis. Leg swelling, recent immobility, surgery, active cancer and previous venous thromboembolism are commonly discussed risk factors. Structured clinical probability assessment and D-dimer testing or imaging such as CT pulmonary angiography are used according to local pathways.

## Aortic dissection considerations
Sudden severe tearing pain radiating to the back, pulse or blood-pressure differences between arms and neurological deficits are features associated with aortic dissection. It is an important consideration because some treatments for other causes of chest pain could be harmful.

## Other causes
Pericarditis may cause sharp pain that changes with position and breathing. Gastro-oesophageal reflux can cause burning retrosternal discomfort related to meals or lying flat. Musculoskeletal chest pain is often reproducible on palpation, but this finding does not by itself exclude serious causes.
