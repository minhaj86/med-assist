# Acute headache warning features (demonstration)

## Overview
Most headaches are primary headaches such as migraine or tension-type headache. Assessment focuses on identifying warning features that suggest a secondary cause requiring urgent evaluation.

## Sudden severe (thunderclap) headache
A headache that reaches maximum intensity within seconds to minutes, often described as the worst headache ever experienced, is a widely recognised warning feature. Subarachnoid haemorrhage is an important consideration. Neck stiffness, vomiting, altered consciousness and collapse at onset may be associated. Brain imaging such as CT head is typically used early, and further testing such as lumbar puncture may be considered according to local pathways and timing.

## Fever, neck stiffness and photophobia
The combination of fever, headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, rash or altered mental state raises concern for meningitis or encephalitis. Blood cultures and specialist pathways are typically considered urgently.

## Focal neurological deficit
New weakness, speech disturbance or visual loss with headache can indicate stroke or other intracranial pathology and generally requires urgent assessment.

## Primary headache features
Migraine commonly causes recurrent unilateral throbbing headache with nausea, photophobia and sometimes aura. Tension-type headache is often bilateral, pressing and without prominent associated features. A similar previous pattern supports, but does not prove, a primary headache.
