# Infection, hypotension and new confusion (demonstration)

## Overview
In patients with suspected infection, signs of organ dysfunction such as low blood pressure, raised respiratory rate, new confusion or reduced urine output are discussed as features that may indicate sepsis. Early recognition and escalation according to local sepsis pathways is emphasised.

## Older adults and atypical presentation
Older adults may present with new confusion, falls or general deterioration rather than localising symptoms. Urinary tract infection and pneumonia are common sources discussed in this context, but new confusion also has non-infective causes such as stroke, hypoglycaemia, medication effects and metabolic disturbance.

## Information commonly used in assessment
Vital sign trends, level of consciousness, lactate, blood cultures before antimicrobials where this does not cause delay, full blood count, renal function, urinalysis and chest imaging are frequently mentioned. Medication review is relevant because some medicines influence presentation or risk.

## Distinguishing features
A clear infective source, fever or hypothermia and raised inflammatory markers support an infective cause. Focal neurological deficits suggest considering stroke. A collateral history from family or carers often clarifies the baseline mental state.
